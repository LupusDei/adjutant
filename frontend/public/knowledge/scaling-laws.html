THE GAUGE THAT WON'T MOVE

Below is a control panel for a mammal. Drag the mass dial from a 1.8 gram shrew to a 150 tonne whale and every readout swings across five orders of magnitude.

Every readout but one. One gauge is stuck. It isn't broken — and the reason it isn't is most of what's known about how life scales.

Why it locks

Heart rate falls as mass to the −1/4. Lifespan rises as mass to the +1/4. They are the same exponent with opposite signs, so when you multiply them to get total beats, the mass cancels out completely and leaves a bare constant. A shrew spends its allowance in eighteen months at twenty beats a second; an elephant spreads the same allowance over sixty-five years. Nobody gets more. They only choose the tempo.

02 / the cost of getting the exponent wrong

One elephant, three exponents

1962. MK Ultra wants to know what LSD does to an elephant. They know the safe dose in a cat: 0.3 mg at 3 kg. Tusko weighs 3,000 kg — a thousand cats. So how much do you give him? Pick the exponent and find out.

DOSE FOR TUSKO

0.3 mg × 1000^α
300 mg

The reasoning that killed him

A thousand times the mass is a thousand times the cells, so a thousand times the dose. It is arithmetic, and it is wrong, because a drug is cleared at the speed the body runs, not the speed it weighs. Within five minutes Tusko trumpeted, collapsed onto his right side and went into status epilepticus. He died shortly after.

Why running ≠ weighing

Burn energy and you make heat, and heat leaves through skin. Volume grows as r³ but surface only as r². Ten times the radius is a thousand times the heat and a hundred times the window to lose it through. An animal on linear scaling boils alive. So metabolism cannot be linear — and everything downstream of metabolism inherits the bend.

03 / the same data, three beliefs

Three laws that agree about a cat

Power laws are straight lines on log–log axes and the slope is the exponent. All three candidate laws are pinned to the same cat. Watch what a thousandfold in mass does to the disagreement.

Drag the console dial above — the vertical marker follows. The gap between the lines at your mass is printed on the plot. At the cat it is nothing. At the elephant it is a factor of ten, and a factor of ten is an elephant's life.

04 / where 1/4 comes from

Life is four-dimensional

The suspicious thing was never 3/4. It was that everything lands on a quarter — lifespan +1/4, heart rate −1/4, brain size 3/4, aorta radius 3/8, lung area 11/12. Quarters imply a four somewhere.

West, Brown and Enquist (1997) found it in the plumbing. Assume only three things: the supply network must reach every cell; the last capillary is the same width in a mouse and an elephant; evolution minimised the cost of pumping. Optimal design then forces a self-similar branching fractal — and a sufficiently crumpled surface stops behaving two-dimensionally.

crumple a 2D sheet→it fills a 3D ball→ exchange area ~ L³→volume = area × length ~ L⁴→ mass ~ L⁴⇒L ~ M1/4

Three spatial dimensions plus one fractal one. Feed L ~ M^(1/4) back into metabolism and Kleiber's 3/4 falls out. That is the whole trick: the quarters are a fourth dimension hiding in the vascular tree.

Why anyone believed it

Fitting one exponent is cheap. WBE's table makes 26 predictions, including ones nobody would reverse-engineer: aorta radius M3/8 = 0.375 against a measured 0.36, lung area M11/12 = 0.92 against a measured 0.95. Sticking your neck out that far and surviving is what a good theory looks like. It is still not proof — rival theories reproduce some of the same numbers, and matching exponents does not certify a mechanism.

Bars are log-scaled to fit. The numbers are the real multiples.

Sublinear ≈ 0.85 — the bargain

Roads, cables, pipe, gas stations. Double the city and you need only about 80% more of them. A shared road carries you and me both. Make a city a hundred times bigger and each person needs roughly half the infrastructure — which makes density, counterintuitively, the green option.

Superlinear ≈ 1.15 — the dividend

Wages, GDP, patents. Double the city and you get about 120% more. A hundredfold city produces roughly 200× the output — double per person. People are measurably faster walkers there too. The city is a machine for putting people within reach of each other.

The sting

Serious crime sits on slope 1.15. So does waste water. So did AIDS cases. The harms share an exponent with the wages. There is no version of the city that keeps the patents and drops the crime, because both are the same phenomenon measured twice: more people within reach of each other. Half the infrastructure per person, double the output per person, double the harm per person — one dial, three consequences.

And unlike biology, nobody knows why. WBE at least offers a mechanism for 3/4. For 0.85 and 1.15 there is no accepted theory at all — the regularity is solid and the explanation is missing. That gap is one of the open problems in the field.

06 / the one mammal that cheated

We get three billion

Three centuries ago humans sat on the curve with everyone else at about a billion beats. Today the average human gets close to three.

Nothing about our biology changed. Germ theory and sanitation arrived in the mid-1800s, child mortality collapsed, and life expectancy climbed — with a visible notch for the 1918 influenza and another for the Second World War. Measured in years, we now live like a mammal somewhere between an elephant and a whale.

The tell is that captive animals do it too. Take a mammal away from predation, starvation and infection and it also overshoots its allowance. So this is not a human-biology exception — it is a hazard exception. The allometry describes the machine. It never described the world the machine has to survive.

Which is the most defensible argument for science anyone has made with a single number: it handed the average person more than one extra life.

07 / what is actually in doubt

The famous number is not settled

Everything above is usually told as finished science. It isn't, and the honest version is more interesting.

Against 3/4

  • Kleiber's dataset was small and mammal-heavy.
  • A 391-species study fits 3/4 only at the large end — smaller mammals track closer to 2/3.
  • Recent bird-metabolism studies also lean 2/3.
  • Measuring metabolic rate requires a large animal to sit resting and unstressed in a box while you read its oxygen precisely. Many studies produce error bars that straddle both 2/3 and 3/4.
  • At a 1960s symposium the field voted 29–0 for 3/4. Some of that number's authority is convention, not data.
  • Peter Dodds (Vermont): the analysis is wrong, or the data too noisy to carry it.

Against 2/3

  • Cold-blooded animals come in well above 2/3 in recent work.
  • WBE's wider exponent table tracks measurement across many unrelated traits, which a pure surface law does not explain.

Where it stands

The community is split, and a growing group suspects there is no universal exponent at all — plausibly ~2/3 for small mammals and ~3/4 for large. The scaling laws are real. The number on them is still being argued.

The actual open action

The measurements have never been redone properly. A century of argument rests on data that a modern lab could improve in a season, and largely nobody has. As one researcher put it: it has to be more than one elephant at one zoo.

08 / what to keep

Five things worth more than the billion

  • A power law is a straight line on log–log, and the slope is the exponent. Below 1 is economies of scale, above 1 is increasing returns, exactly 1 is the naive default that is usually wrong.
  • Find a power law, look for self-similarity. The exponent is nearly always a property of some network you hadn't thought about.
  • Equal and opposite exponents make an invariant — and the invariant is usually the interesting quantity. That is where the billion came from.
  • Being big is often a bargain. A hundredfold organism needs only 31.6× the energy; a hundredfold city needs only 50× the pipe. Sometimes things punch above their weight.
  • Before extrapolating across orders of magnitude, ask what the quantity is actually limited by. Tusko died of a units error wearing the costume of arithmetic.

Bonus, for the next time the pace of life feels unsurvivable: "Everything nowadays is ultra … young people are stirred up much too early and carried away in the whirl of the times." Goethe, 1825. Every generation has felt it. Every generation has managed.

And a domestic one, straight from the surface law: cooking time goes as mass2/3. Double the roast, cook it about 60% longer — not twice as long.
